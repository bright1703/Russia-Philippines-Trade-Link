Issue of 22 October 2026

What this means. The pork file is the one to watch: Manila is weighing a pause on import clearances, running a safeguard investigation and reviewing tariffs, while quota applications for next year are already open. Suppliers should first confirm with their Philippine buyer that a clearance is already issued before booking any shipment. Issue of 19 October 2026

What this means. The items in this digest are scattered, but on pork they point the same way: duties are set to rise while existing stocks will already hold back buying for the coming months and into next year. Pork suppliers should confirm the rate and the timing with local importers before committing shipments. For perishables, the more relevant move is to check which partners will get the new cold storage capacity and on what terms they accept cargo. Rice purchases are rising on weather risk and mostly concern Asian suppliers, while the overall backdrop is that food prices remain the main concern for buyers, so price is the first thing an importer will ask about.

Issue of 1 October 2026

What this means. The items are a mixed bag: steel, poultry and pork rules, rice and corn supply, inflation and infrastructure. The one that matters most is food, since the Philippines will keep buying rice and corn abroad while costly transport and a weak peso squeeze importers, so check whether the corn import quota is widened and who already holds those volumes, as Argentina is pushing in. Steel runs the other way, with a long-term plan to cut import reliance, so the better angle is equipment and services for new mills, storage and the metro rather than finished steel. Also track the reclassification of pork cheeks and updates on poultry bans, since those decisions can change duties and market access with little notice.
