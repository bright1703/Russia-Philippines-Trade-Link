GUIDES FOR PHILIPPINE IMPORTERS

Shipping from Vladivostok to the Philippines

Routes, transit times, trade terms and the checks to make before you book a container from the Russian Far East.

Information guide, not legal advice. Sources checked on 27 October 2026.

Short answer. As of 27 October 2026 we found no published direct container service between Vladivostok and a Philippine port. Cargo usually changes ships at a hub such as Shanghai, Ningbo or Busan. One forwarder’s published table shows about 23 days from Vladivostok to Manila via Shanghai. Before you book, agree the trade term, check the carrier and vessel with your bank, and have permits and a customs broker ready before the ship sails.

Expect a transshipment, not a direct ship

Vladivostok is the main container gateway of the Russian Far East. Its regular lines run mostly to China, Korea, Japan and Vietnam.

FESCO, the largest carrier in the port, lists services from Vladivostok to Ho Chi Minh City, Haiphong, several Chinese ports, Busan and Japan. No Philippine port appears on its line list. FESCO container lines

Korean carrier Sinokor added a service in March 2026 linking Haiphong, Shekou, Xiamen and Busan with Vladivostok. The report describes Busan as a hub for short-sea routes. The Loadstar, 16 March 2026

A Vladivostok forwarder publishes a transit table that includes Vladivostok to Manila via Shanghai in 23 days. The same page warns that schedules and transit times may change. Hub Shipping service table

In practice, a container leaves Vladivostok on one vessel, waits at a hub in China or Korea, then continues to Manila or another Philippine port on a second vessel. Each change of ship adds waiting time and another handling.

Rotations change often. FESCO revised its China services in February 2026 and again in October 2026. Container News, 5 October 2026

Ask the supplier or forwarder for the full routing in writing. It should name the carrier, each vessel, the transshipment port and the estimated dates for every leg.

Plan the time realistically

A published transit time covers the sea voyage between terminals under normal conditions. It does not include the time the supplier needs to obtain an empty container, load it and complete Russian export formalities.

It also excludes any wait for a connecting vessel at the hub and the time needed for Philippine customs and agency clearance after arrival.

Do not promise your own customers a delivery date based on the sea leg alone. Agree on a shipment date in the contract and track the container through the carrier.

Agree who books the freight and when risk passes

Write the trade term into the contract with a named place and the edition, for example “FCA Vladivostok, [terminal name], Incoterms 2020” or “CFR Manila, Incoterms 2020”. Incoterms are eleven three-letter rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce. ICC Incoterms rules

Under FOB, CFR and CIF, risk passes to the buyer when the goods are on board the vessel at the port of loading. A container is usually handed to the terminal earlier, so many traders use FCA for container cargo instead.

ICC revised FCA in Incoterms 2020 for sea shipments where a bank or the parties need a bill of lading with an on-board notation. ICC, Incoterms 2020

The term also decides who chooses the carrier.

  • FCA or FOB. You book the freight. You control the carrier and route, but you need a forwarder that accepts cargo from Russia.
  • CFR or CIF. The supplier books the freight. This is simpler for a first order, but you have less say over the carrier, the vessel and the transshipment port.

Whichever term you choose, ask for the carrier and vessel names before the booking is confirmed. Your bank may need them, as explained below.

Check the carrier and the vessel, not only the supplier

Banks and insurers often screen every party named in the shipping documents. This can include the shipping line, the vessel and the port operator.

Far Eastern Shipping Company (FESCO) has been on the UK sanctions list since 18 May 2023, with an asset freeze and transport sanctions. UK sanctions list

Interfax reported that PJSC FESCO was added to EU sanctions in October 2025. Interfax, 6 November 2025 Our name search of the US OFAC SDN list on 27 October 2026 found no entry for the company.

A Philippine company is not directly bound by UK or EU sanctions. A bank, insurer or hub operator that applies them may still refuse or delay a payment, a policy or a transshipment connected with a listed company. Our guide on whether it is legal to import from Russia explains how these rules reach Philippine businesses.

Send the proposed carrier and vessel names to your bank together with the supplier details. Do this before you agree on payment terms. Our guide on how to pay a Russian supplier lists what else the bank may ask for.

Prepare for Philippine customs before the ship sails

Importers and customs brokers must be accredited with the Bureau of Customs. Entries are lodged electronically through the BOC system, and some hard copies are still submitted to the entry processing unit. US Commercial Service, Philippines customs regulations, 30 June 2026

The same source lists the core documents for an import entry.

  • Commercial invoice or other document showing payment
  • Bill of lading
  • Packing list
  • Notarized supplemental declaration on valuation
  • Import permits or clearances where the goods are regulated

Shipping lines and forwarders must send cargo information to the BOC electronically within 24 hours after the vessel leaves the port of origin. This applies to the first vessel and to any feeder vessel bound for the Philippines. BOC CAO 01-2016

The goods description, weights and consignee on the bill of lading should match the invoice and packing list. Check the draft bill of lading before the supplier releases it.

Russia and the Eurasian Economic Union do not appear among the Philippines’ free trade agreements. Goods from Russia therefore do not get FTA preference rates. Check the general duty for your HS code in the Tariff Commission’s tariff finder. US Commercial Service, Philippines trade agreements

Food, meat, plant products, fertilizer and other regulated goods need permits from the responsible Philippine agency. Some must be in place before the goods are shipped. Check the requirements for your product in the Philippine National Trade Repository and with the agency before the container is loaded.

Wood pallets and crates need an ISPM 15 mark

The Bureau of Plant Industry regulates wood packaging such as pallets, crates and dunnage under the international ISPM 15 standard. Its order requires the approved mark on at least two opposite sides. BPI order on wood packaging, 2004, filed with IPPC

Under that order, the importer applies for inspection at least 24 hours before arrival. Cargo with unmarked wood packaging is held. The packaging is treated, or destroyed if treatment is not available. If live pests are found, the cargo is treated at the importer’s expense or returned to the country of origin.

Packaging made only of plywood, particle board or OSB is outside the scope. BPI revised the order in 2010, and we could not open the full revised text. Confirm the current procedure with your customs broker.

Ask the supplier to confirm in writing that all wood packaging is ISPM 15 treated and marked.

Frozen and chilled cargo

Reefer containers must stay powered at every terminal, including the transshipment hub. Each change of vessel is a point where the cold chain can break.

State the set temperature in the booking and on the bill of lading. Ask for the container’s temperature record after arrival. Agree in the contract what happens if the record shows a deviation.

Insure the cargo from the point where risk passes

Under CIF, the seller only has to buy minimum insurance cover. Check whether that cover is enough for the value and type of goods. ICC, Incoterms 2020

Under FCA or FOB, arrange your own cargo insurance from the moment risk passes to you. Tell the insurer that the goods are of Russian origin and name the carrier. Ask whether this affects cover or claims.

Before you book a container

  • The full routing is in writing, with the carrier, vessels, transshipment port and estimated dates
  • Your bank has reviewed the supplier, the carrier and the vessel
  • The contract names the Incoterms rule, the place and the edition
  • Import permits for regulated goods are approved or the timing is confirmed
  • Your company or broker is accredited with the Bureau of Customs
  • The supplier has confirmed ISPM 15 marking for any wood packaging
  • Cargo insurance starts at the point where risk passes to you
  • You have checked the draft bill of lading against the invoice and packing list

Frequently asked questions

Is there a direct ship from Vladivostok to Manila?

As of 27 October 2026 we found no published direct container service. Cargo usually changes vessels at a hub in China or Korea. Ask the carrier for the current routing, because services change often.

How long does shipping from Vladivostok to Manila take?

One Vladivostok forwarder’s table shows about 23 days via Shanghai for the sea voyage. Add time for loading, export formalities, waiting at the hub and clearance in the Philippines.

Which Incoterms rule should I use for a container?

Many traders use FCA for containers, because the box is handed to the terminal before it is loaded on the vessel. FOB, CFR and CIF pass risk only when the goods are on board. Agree the rule with the supplier and your bank.

Can my bank object to the shipping line?

Yes. Banks and insurers may screen the carrier and vessel named in the shipping documents. Send these names to your bank before you confirm the booking or the payment terms.
